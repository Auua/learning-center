## Foreword

***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu*** (Japanese for Everyone – Intermediate) has been planned and edited to form a comprehensive set of Japanese-language learning materials following on from ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu I*** and ***II*** (Japanese for Everyone – Introductory I & II), which were first published in 1998. The ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu*** materials were originally developed to enable general adult beginners to acquire an introductory level of Japanese-language ability rapidly, but they are also widely used as introductory textbooks both in Japan and abroad by non-Japanese students either already studying at Japanese universities and colleges or preparing to do so.

Meanwhile, the number of non-Japanese intending to work in Japan or already living here continues to increase as the Japanese birthrate falls and international interchange gains pace, and the ***Minna no Nihongo*** books are also popular among such people because of the way the materials help them to learn Japanese easily.

As the range of people using the ***Minna no Nihongo*** books widened and demand for them increased, we began to receive requests from many different quarters for a set of intermediate materials that would follow on from ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu I*** and ***II***. After extensive writing, testing and editing, we are now finally able to present everyone with what they have been asking for — ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu***.

At the introductory level, people who need to communicate in Japanese are required to be able to say what they want to say and understand what is said to them. At the intermediate level, however, I believe they need more than this — they also need sufficiently good language skills to be able to understand and explore Japanese culture and customs. This book is designed to give ample support to learners like these.

Finally, I should like to thank everyone sincerely for their valuable opinions and for all the help they gave us in preparing and testing these materials. We at 3A Corporation intend to continue contributing to people's networking through activities such as developing and publishing the educational materials that today's multicultural, interdependent community requires, and I sincerely hope that you will continue to support and encourage us in this.

May 2009

## Explanatory Notes

### I. Structure of the Materials

The ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I*** materials consist of a Main Text (with CD) and the associated Translation and Grammar Notes (to be made available separately in various languages, starting with an English version). The materials aim to help learners develop a comprehensive set of Japanese-language skills in speaking/listening and reading/writing at the lower Intermediate level (enabling them to progress from introductory to intermediate level Japanese). ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I*** will be followed by ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu II***, which will enable learners to develop their skills further and ultimately achieve a mastery of intermediate-level Japanese.

### II. Contents and Method of Use

#### 1. Main Text (with CD)

**(1) Lessons**

***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I*** contains twelve lessons and follows on from ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu I*** and ***II*** (which contained a total of fifty lessons). They consist of:

**1) Grammar and Practice Drills**

The grammar points introduced in each lesson are presented in the form of sentence patterns. Grammar terminology is avoided.

When a sentence is connected to a sentence pattern, it is indicated by「…」,
　e.g.「…ということだ」(Lesson 2).

When a noun or noun phrase is connected to a sentence pattern, it is indicated by「～」,
　e.g.「～を～と{言|い}う」(Lesson 1).

However, even if a connection is a sentence, it is indicated by「～」if its ending requires a special form such as the て-form, the た-form, the dictionary form, the たら-form, the ている-form, or the ば-form,
　e.g.「～たら、～た」(Lesson 2).

The ways in which the grammar points (sentence patterns) are actually used are illustrated by example sentences and dialogues. Practice drills are provided to help learners develop their ability to use the grammar points, and illustrations are given whenever necessary to show the context. Learners are encouraged to make their own statements based on the sentence-pattern drills, which are designed to develop conversational topics and thereby strengthen conversational and reading comprehension skills.

**2) Speak and Listen**

Communication scenes are set out (mainly discussions and negotiations about everyday topics), and model dialogues and graded exercises (described below) are provided. By working through these exercises, which are designed to stimulate interest and increase learning motivation, learners ultimately become able to use the intended dialogues without relying on rote memorization. The characters appearing in the dialogues (old friends from ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu I*** and ***II***) converse in a variety of situations.

1. やってみましょう (Let's Try It)<br>This introduces the target dialogue. Learners use the Japanese they already know to make conversation in the situation described, in accordance with the set tasks.
2. {聞|き}いてみましょう (Let's Listen)<br>Learners listen to the dialogue on the CD, paying close attention to the key listening points and expressions introduced in each lesson.
3. もう{一度|いちど}{聞|き}きましょう (Let's Listen Again)<br>Learners complete their comprehension of the dialogue by filling in the blanks in the exercises while listening to the dialogue on the CD again (playing it back in a way that matches their level of understanding of the material).
4. {言|い}ってみましょう (Let's Try Saying It)<br>Learners try repeating the dialogue as it is on the CD, focusing on their pronunciation and intonation.
5. {練習|れんしゅう}をしましょう (Let's Practice)<br>Learners practice the words and expressions used in the dialogue.
6. {会話|かいわ}をしましょう (Let's Talk)<br>Learners practice the dialogue by recreating it while looking at the illustrations.
7. チャレンジしましょう (Let's Challenge Ourselves)<br>After understanding the situation and relationships described, learners practice the negotiation dialogue targeted in the lesson.

**3) Read and Write**

In「{読|よ}みましょう」('Let's Read'), twelve different reading texts selected to pique readers' interest and enable them to enjoy reading have been provided.

1. {考|かんが}えてみましょう (Let's Think About It)<br>To prepare themselves to read the text, learners activate their knowledge of the topic.
2. ことばをチェックしましょう (Let's Check the Words)<br>Learners do this exercise to make sure they know the key words (including new vocabulary) that they need in order to understand the text. They should check the meaning of any words they do not know in the new vocabulary list or a dictionary.
3. {読|よ}みましょう (Let's Read)<br>Each lesson's reading text is accompanied by「{読|よ}むときのポイント」(Points to Note When Reading), which sets tasks designed to have learners practice the skills and strategies they need in order to understand the text. The aim is to enable them to obtain an accurate understanding of the gist of the text rapidly.<br>A text can be read silently or aloud, but the latter is also considered important here, and the CD contains specific examples of vocalized expressions for this purpose.
4. {答|こた}えましょう (Let's Answer)<br>Learners do this exercise to check whether they have correctly accomplished the tasks set in 'Points to Note When Reading.' Where necessary, there are also questions on the detailed contents of the texts.
5. チャレンジしましょう (Let's Challenge Ourselves)<br>The aim of this exercise is to enable learners to speak or write about their own experiences or familiar events relating to the contents of the reading text.

**4) Revision Exercises**

The Revision Exercises include ones for listening comprehension (indicated by the CD symbol 🔊), grammar, and vocabulary. The listening comprehension ones are of two types: listening to the CD and answering short questions, and listening to a short dialogue and grasping its key points. Both of these apply the key learning points introduced in the lesson and are designed to reinforce learners' listening comprehension. The grammar exercises check learners' understanding of the new sentence patterns introduced in each lesson, and the vocabulary exercises focus on enabling learners to remember and apply functional words.

**(2) Key Learning Points**

**1) Speak and Listen**
- ① Title of dialogue
- ② Learning goals (strategy)
- ③ Grammar points (sentence patterns) introduced in Speak and Listen (42 points)
- ④ ＊: Supplementary points (9 in total; see Explanatory Notes 2. ***Translation and Grammatical Notes***)

**2) Read and Write**
- ① Title of reading text
- ② Hints for reading (strategy)
- ③ Grammar points (sentence patterns) introduced in Read and Write (53 points)
- ④ ＊: Supplementary points (8 in total; see Explanatory Notes 2. ***Translation and Grammatical Notes***)

**(3) Kanji Usage**

1) As a rule, kanji are selected from the {常用漢字表|じょうようかんじひょう} (the official list of kanji in common use) and its Appendix.
- ① {熟字訓|じゅくじくん} (kanji compounds having a special reading) that appear in the Appendix are written in kanji:<br>e.g. {友達|ともだち} friend　{眼鏡|めがね} spectacles　{二十歳|はたち} twenty years old　{風邪|かぜ} cold
- ② Proper nouns, and artistic, cultural and family-member names, are written in their own kanji even if those kanji do not appear in the official list:<br>e.g. {厳島神社|いつくしまじんじゃ} Itsukushima Shrine　{夏目漱石|なつめそうせき} Natsume Soseki　{姪|めい} niece

2) Some words are written in kana to simplify students' reading, even when their kanji are in the official list or its Appendix:<br>e.g. ある（{有|あ}る・{在|あ}る）be　いまさら（{今更|いまさら}）now　さまざま（{様々|さまざま}）various

3) Numbers are chiefly shown in Arabic numerals:<br>e.g. 9{時|じ} 9 o'clock　10{月|がつ}2{日|ふつか} 2 February　90{歳|さい} 90 years old<br>But kanji are used in cases like the following:<br>{一人|ひとり}で by oneself　{一戸建|いっこだ}て detached house　{一日中|いちにちじゅう} all day long

4) The readings of all the kanji appearing in the Main Text of ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I*** are given in furigana.

**(4) Indexes**
1) New Vocabulary (approx. 910 words)
2) Conversational Expressions (approx. 50)
3) Kanji (315 of the official-list kanji appearing in the reading texts in all 12 lessons; introductory-level kanji omitted)

**(5) Answers**
1) Answers
- ① Grammar and Practice Drills, Speak and Listen, Read and Write
- ② Revision Exercises (including scripts for listening comprehension exercises)<br>Some of the questions will have more than one possible answer, depending on the learner's background. The answers given here are merely model examples.

2) 'Speak and Listen' dialogue scripts
3) Contents of CD

**(6) CD**

The CD contains ① the dialogues from Speak and Listen, ② the reading texts from Read and Write, and ③ the listening comprehension parts of the Revision Exercises.

In addition to studying the pronunciation word by word while paying attention to accent and intonation, learners can use the dialogues to accustom themselves to Japanese spoken at normal speed, as well as to develop their ability to understand the key points in the flow of a conversation and answer questions about it.

Also, when listening to the reading texts, they can enjoy hearing the richness of Japanese expressions, which vary with the genre of the writing. They should pay attention to how the different parts of the text are read, and what rhythm and tone variation are used.

Finally, by using the Revision Exercises to check what they have learned, students can organize their thoughts and develop an all-round ability to apply their knowledge when speaking and writing.

#### 2. Translation and Grammatical Notes (available as separate volumes in various languages)

These cover lessons 1 through 12 and contain the following:

**(1) New Vocabulary and Its Translation**

New words, conversational expressions and proper nouns are given in the order they appear in the lessons.

**(2) Grammatical Notes**

1) Grammar Points<br>The grammar points (sentence patterns) in each lesson have been compiled from the grammar syllabus considered necessary for intermediate-level learners.

2) Grammar Explanations (in various languages)<br>Explanations of the grammar points have been kept to the minimum needed by learners. Example sentences are used to clarify their meaning and function and indicate when and in what actual situations they can be used.

3) Connections<br>In the Main Text, thought has been given to making the connections understandable without using technical grammar terminology, by presenting grammar points in the form of sentence patterns and illustrating them with example sentences.<br>In each language version of the Grammatical Notes, the forms of all the connections are shown, so that learners can check them for themselves. Grammar terminology is also used where necessary.

4) References and Supplementary Notes<br>A second language is acquired not just by building up sequentially from the introductory stage, but also by working in spiral fashion (combining new grammar with previously-studied grammar, so that material already learned can be repeated and reinforced). The References show points introduced in ***Minna no Nihongo Shokyu*** and other relevant points. The Supplementary Notes (in the Key Learning Points at the end of the Main Text) list points which, while not appearing in the Grammar and Practice Drills sections of the Main Text, are nevertheless considered useful for learners to know.

## How to Use This Textbook Effectively

These notes explain the key points that learners should bear in mind in order to make most effective use of the ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I Main Text*** (with CD) and the associated ***Translation and Grammatical Notes*** (available separately in various languages).

### I. *Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I Main Text* (with CD)

#### 1. Grammar and Practice Drills

For each grammar point, begin by looking at the example sentences to see in what circumstances and situations the relevant sentence patterns and expressions can be used. Also compare these with elementary sentence patterns and expressions that can be used to express similar things. Then check the connections, do the Practice Drills, and try using what you have learned in actual speaking, listening, reading and writing situations.

#### 2. Speak and Listen (Dialogue)

First, do Exercise 1「やってみましょう」('Let's Try It'), trying to create a conversation using the Japanese you already know. Then do Exercise 2「{聞|き}いてみましょう」('Let's Listen'), listening to the dialogue on the CD while paying close attention to the wording. Then do Exercise 3「もう{一度|いちど}{聞|き}きましょう」('Let's Listen Again'), filling in the blanks while listening to the dialogue on the CD once more. Then do Exercise 4「{言|い}ってみましょう」('Let's Try Saying It'), speaking the dialogue out loud along with the CD while paying attention to your pronunciation and intonation. Then do Exercise 5「{練習|れんしゅう}をしましょう」('Let's Practice'), further practicing the expressions from the dialogue. Finally, do Exercise 6「{会話|かいわ}をしましょう」('Let's Talk'), making up a conversation based on the pictures.

If you practice like this, you will become able to make conversation naturally, without having to force yourself to learn, and you will easily be able to extend your skills by doing Exercise 7「チャレンジしましょう」('Let's Challenge Ourselves').

The dialogue scripts can be found in the「{解答|かいとう}」(Answers) section at the end of the book.

#### 3. Read and Write (Reading Text)

Before reading the text, please get ready. Do Exercise 1「{考|かんが}えてみましょう」('Let's Think About It'), thinking about the topic of the text and discussing it with your classmates and teacher. Then do Exercise 2「ことばをチェックしましょう」('Let's Check the Words'), reviewing the words that appear in the text and looking up any unfamiliar ones in a dictionary or in the New Vocabulary list in ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I Translation and Grammatical Notes*** (available separately in various languages).

Next, do Exercise 3「{読|よ}みましょう」('Let's Read'), reading the text yourself. Follow the instructions in the「{読|よ}むときのポイント」('Points to Note When Reading') box, as you will need these in order to understand the text.

Then do Exercise 4「{答|こた}えましょう」('Let's Answer'), to check how well you have understood the text. Finally do Exercise 5「チャレンジしましょう」('Let's Challenge Ourselves'), putting the finishing touches to your reading comprehension by telling others what you know about or have experienced concerning the topic of the text, writing about it, and so on.

There is a Kanji Index at the end of the book which gives the 315 {常用漢字表|じょうようかんじひょう} kanji appearing in this book (not including elementary ones) in their order of appearance. This will be a useful resource for learning how to read and write kanji and studying their meanings and uses.

#### 4. Revision Exercises

Do these Revision Exercises to ensure that you have properly understood the meanings and usage of the grammar points and vocabulary introduced in the lesson. The answers are at the end of the book.

#### 5. CD (🔊: CD symbol)

Each lesson on the CD contains ① the dialogues from Speak and Listen, ② the reading texts from Read and Write and ③ the listening comprehension parts of the Revision Exercises.

- 🔊 Dialogue: The speed of the dialogues increases little by little with each successive lesson. Use them to get used to Japanese spoken at natural speed, and to practice grasping the key points of the conversations.
- 🔊 Reading Text: As you listen to the CD, pay attention to the rhythm and tone, as well as to how each part of the text is read.
- 🔊 Revision Exercises: Check your listening comprehension by doing these exercises, which introduce different applications of what you have learned in the lesson.

### II. *Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I Translation and Grammatical Notes* (available separately in various languages)

These consist of New Vocabulary and Grammatical Notes.

#### 1. New Vocabulary

New vocabulary, conversational expressions and proper nouns are given in the order in which they appear in the lessons. Check how they are used in the Main Text, and develop your ability to use and apply them by repeatedly practicing them together with the approximately 2,000 terms introduced in the introductory books.

#### 2. Grammatical Notes

These explain the grammar of the approximately 100 grammar points (sentence patterns) appearing in the 'Speak and Listen' (dialogue) and 'Read and Write' (reading text) sections of each lesson. Improve your ability to use these by studying their meaning and function and deepening your understanding of them in actual conversational situations or in the context of the reading texts.

The ***Minna no Nihongo Chukyu I*** text has been designed to help learners make a smooth and enjoyable transition from introductory-level to intermediate-level Japanese, with the four functions (speaking, listening, reading and writing) of words and expressions introduced in a balanced way. We hope that they will help learners to develop their Japanese-language abilities to the lower intermediate level and build a sound platform for their next step of progressing to the higher intermediate level.

## Lesson 1

#### 1．～てもらえませんか・～ていただけませんか・～てもらえないでしょうか・～ていただけないでしょうか

**Connection:** Vて-form ＋ もらえませんか／いただけませんか／もらえないでしょうか／いただけないでしょうか

「～てもらえませんか」and「～ていただけませんか」are used when politely asking someone to do something:

①　ちょっとペンを{貸|か}してもらえませんか。<br>Could you lend me your pen for a moment, please?

②　コピー{機|き}の{使|つか}い{方|かた}を{教|おし}えていただけませんか。<br>Would you show me how to use the copier, please?

> **Ref:** 「～ていただけませんか（polite request）」：<br>いい{先生|せんせい}を{紹介|しょうかい}していただけませんか。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 26）

「～てもらえないでしょうか」and「～ていただけないでしょうか」sound even politer and softer than「～てもらえませんか」and「～ていただけませんか」:

③　すみません、{子|こ}どもが{寝|ね}ているので、もう{少|すこ}し{静|しず}かにしてもらえないでしょうか。<br>Excuse me, my child's asleep, so would you mind being a little quieter, please?

④　{申|もう}し{訳|わけ}ございませんが、{子|こ}どもを{預|あず}かっていただけないでしょうか。<br>I'm very sorry to trouble you, but could you possibly look after my child for me?

#### 2．～のようだ・～のような～・～のように…（simile; illustration）

**Connection:** Nの ＋ ようだ／ようなN／ようにV・いA・なA

「～のようだ」is used at the end of a sentence when describing the characteristics of one noun (N₁) by likening it to another noun (N₂) (a simile):

①　あの{病院|びょういん}はホテルのようだ。<br>That hospital is like a hotel.

②　このお{酒|さけ}はジュースのようだ。<br>This cocktail tastes like juice.

「のようだ」is changed into「のような」when modifying a noun:

③　{田中|たなか}さんはホテルのような{病院|びょういん}に{入院|にゅういん}している。<br>Mr. Tanaka is in a hospital like a hotel.

④　わたしはジュースのようなお{酒|さけ}しか{飲|の}まない。<br>I only drink cocktails that taste like juice.

「のようだ」is changed into「のように」when modifying a verb or an adjective:

⑤　{田中|たなか}さんが{入院|にゅういん}している{病院|びょういん}はホテルのようにきれいだ。<br>The hospital Mr. Tanaka is in is as nice as a hotel.

⑥　このお{酒|さけ}はジュースのように{甘|あま}い。<br>This cocktail tastes as sweet as juice.

「のような」is mostly used to describe something by citing N₂ as an example (illustration):

⑦　{夫|おっと}は、カレーのような{簡単|かんたん}な{料理|りょうり}しか{作|つく}れません。<br>My husband can only cook simple dishes like curry.

⑧　「アポ」のような{外来語|がいらいご}は、{外国人|がいこくじん}にはとても{難|むずか}しい。<br>Loanwords like 'apo' are very difficult for non-Japanese people.

> **Ref:** 「…ようだ（guessing from a situation）」：<br>{人|ひと}が{大勢|おおぜい}{集|あつ}まっていますね。…{事故|じこ}のようですね。パトカーと{救急車|きゅうきゅうしゃ}が{来|き}ていますよ。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 47）

#### 3．～ことは／が／を

**Connection:** V dic.-form ＋ こと ＋ は／が／を

Adding「～こと」turns a verb into a gerund;「こと」is used to nominalize the verb:

①　{朝|あさ}{早|はや}く{起|お}きることは{健康|けんこう}にいい。<br>Getting up early in the morning is good for the health.

②　{田中|たなか}さんは{踊|おど}ることが{好|す}きです。<br>Mr. Tanaka likes dancing.

③　{優勝|ゆうしょう}することを{目指|めざ}しています。<br>I'm aiming to win the championship.

> **Ref:** 「V dic.-form ＋ ことができます／ことです」<br>わたしはピアノを{弾|ひ}くことができます。<br>わたしの{趣味|しゅみ}は{映画|えいが}を{見|み}ることです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 18）

#### 4．～を～と{言|い}う

**Connection:** N₁をN₂と{言|い}う

「～を～と{言|い}う」is used when telling someone the name (N₂) of a thing or an event (N₁).

①　1{月|がつ}1{日|ついたち}を{元日|がんじつ}と{言|い}います。<br>We call the 1st of January 'Ganjitsu' (New Year's Day).

②　{正月|しょうがつ}に{神社|じんじゃ}やお{寺|てら}に{行|い}くことを{初詣|はつもう}でと{言|い}う。<br>We call visiting a shrine or temple at New Year 'Hatsumode.'

#### 5．～という～

**Connection:** N₁というN₂

「～という～」is used to quote the name or title of a thing or person that the listener/reader may not know about. N₁ is a person's name or other proper noun, while N₂ is a common noun:

①　{夏目漱石|なつめそうせき}という{小説家|しょうせつか}を{知|し}っていますか。<br>Have you heard of the novelist called Natsume Soseki?

②　{昨日|きのう}、「スター・ウォーズ」という{映画|えいが}を{見|み}ました。<br>Yesterday I saw a film called 'Star Wars.'

#### 6．いつ／どこ／{何|なに}／だれ／どんなに～ても

**Connection:** Vて-form／いA-くて／なA・N＋で ＋ も

These express something that happens under all circumstances, whatever they might be. Use the form「ても」after「いつ」,「どこ」,「{何|なに}」,「だれ」,「どんなに」, etc.:

①　{世界中|せかいじゅう}どこにいても{家族|かぞく}のことを{忘|わす}れません。<br>Wherever I am in the world, I never forget my family.

②　{何度|なんど}{聞|き}いても{同|おな}じことしか{教|おし}えてくれない。<br>However many times I ask, they only tell me the same thing.

③　だれが{何|なん}と{言|い}っても{考|かんが}えを{変|か}えません。<br>I'm not going to change my opinion, no matter what anyone says.

④　どんなに{高|たか}くても{買|か}いたいです。<br>I want to buy it, regardless of how expensive it might be.

With a noun, the expression takes the form「どんなNでも」,「どのNでも」or「どんなに～Nでも」:

⑤　どんな{人|ひと}でも{優|やさ}しい{心|こころ}を{持|も}っているはずだ。<br>I'm convinced that everyone, whoever he may be, is kind at heart.

⑥　{正月|しょうがつ}になると、どの{神社|じんじゃ}でも{人|ひと}がいっぱいだ。<br>When New Year arrives, shrines everywhere are packed with people.

⑦　どんなに{丈夫|じょうぶ}なかばんでも{長|なが}く{使|つか}えば、{壊|こわ}れてしまうこともある。<br>However strong a suitcase might be, it can break if you use it for a long time.

> **Ref:** 「～ても（adversative conjunction）」：いくら{考|かんが}えても、わかりません。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 25）

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

#### ～じゃなくて、～

The expression「N₁じゃなくて、N₂」negates N₁ and proposes N₂ instead:

①　これはペンじゃなくて、チョコレートです。{食|た}べられますよ。<br>This isn't a pen, it's chocolate. It's edible, you know.

②　{京都|きょうと}ではお{寺|てら}を{見|み}ましょうか。<br>…お{寺|てら}じゃなくて、{若|わか}い{人|ひと}が{行|い}くようなにぎやかなところに{行|い}きたいです。<br>Shall we go to see some temples while we're in Kyoto?<br>Not temples. I'd rather go somewhere lively, where young people go.

### {読|よ}む・{書|か}く

#### …のだ・…のではない

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・＊N plain form（－だ→－な） ＋ のだ／＊のではない

「…のです」can be used in the following form when indicating a result that has arisen for a particular reason, or a guess based on certain logical grounds:

①　3{時|じ}の{飛行機|ひこうき}に{乗|の}らなければなりません。それで、わたしは{急|いそ}いでいるのです。<br>I have to get on the 3 o'clock plane, so I'm in a hurry.<br>(reason／logical ground)（だから／それで）(result／guess)

②　{彼|かれ}は{日本|にほん}に{留学|りゅうがく}します。それで{日本語|にほんご}を{勉強|べんきょう}しているのです。<br>He's going to college in Japan. That's why he's learning Japanese.

③　このレポートは{一人|ひとり}で{書|か}いたのではありません。<br>I didn't write this report all by myself.<br>*cf.*　×このレポートは{一人|ひとり}で{書|か}きませんでした。

#### {何人|なんにん}も、{何回|なんかい}も、{何枚|なんまい}も…

「{何|なん}＋counter（{人|にん}、{回|かい}、{枚|まい}…）＋も」indicates a large number of something:

①　マンションの{前|まえ}にパトカーが{何台|なんだい}も{止|と}まっています。<br>There are a lot of police cars parked in front of the apartment block.

## Lesson 2

#### 1．(1)(2)　～たら、～た

**Connection:** Vたら、｛V・A｝た

(1)「XたらYた」indicates that Y happened as a result of an action (X):

①　{薬|くすり}を{飲|の}んだら、{元気|げんき}になりました。<br>I felt better after I had taken the medicine.

②　カーテンを{変|か}えたら、{部屋|へや}が{明|あか}るくなった。<br>The room became brighter after I changed the curtains.

(2) It can also indicate that Y was discovered as a result of an action (X):

③　{家|いえ}に{帰|かえ}ったら、{猫|ねこ}がいなかった。<br>After getting home, I noticed the cat wasn't there.

④　かばんを{開|あ}けたら、{財布|さいふ}がなくなっていた。<br>When I opened my bag, I found that my purse was missing.

⑤　50{年前|ねんまえ}の{古|ふる}いお{酒|さけ}を{飲|の}んでみたら、おいしかった。<br>On trying the fifty-year-old sake, I found it to be delicious.

The same meanings as in (1) and (2) can also be expressed by「Xと、Yた」:

⑥　{薬|くすり}を{飲|の}むと、{元気|げんき}になりました。<br>I felt better after taking the medicine.

⑦　{家|いえ}に{帰|かえ}ると、{猫|ねこ}がいなかった。<br>When I got home, I found the cat wasn't there.

> **Ref:** 「～たら（subjunctive）」：お{金|かね}があったら、{旅行|りょこう}します。<br>「～たら（perfective）」：10{時|じ}になったら、{出|で}かけましょう。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 25）

#### 2．～というのは～のことだ・～というのは…ということだ

**Connection:** Nというのは ＋ ｛Nの／S (plain form) という｝ ＋ ことだ

「Xというのは～のことだ」and「Xというのは…ということだ」are expressions used when explaining the meaning of a term (X).

①　3Kというのはきたない、きつい、{危険|きけん}な{仕事|しごと}のことだ。<br>'3D' means dirty, difficult and dangerous work.

②　PCというのはパソコンのことです。<br>'PC' stands for 'personal computer.'

③　{禁煙|きんえん}というのはたばこを{吸|す}ってはいけないということです。<br>'{禁煙|きんえん}' means that smoking is prohibited.

④　{駐車|ちゅうしゃ}{違反|いはん}というのは{車|くるま}を{止|と}めてはいけない{場所|ばしょ}に{車|くるま}を{止|と}めたということです。<br>'{駐車|ちゅうしゃ}{違反|いはん}' means that you have parked a car in a place where a car should not be parked.

#### 3．…という～

**Connection:** S (plain form) ＋ というN (noun expressing an utterance or thought)

The form「…という～」is used when giving the details of a noun signifying an utterance or thought, such as「{話|はなし}、うわさ、{考|かんが}え、{意見|いけん}、{意志|いし}、{批判|ひはん}、ニュース」('talk,' 'rumor,' 'thought,' 'opinion,' 'intention,' 'criticism,' or 'news'):

①　{昔|むかし}ここは{海|うみ}だったという{話|はなし}を{知|し}っていますか。<br>Did you know that they say this area used to be under the sea?

②　{田中|たなか}さんがもうすぐ{会社|かいしゃ}を{辞|や}めるといううわさを{聞|き}きました。<br>I heard a rumor that Mr. Tanaka is about to leave the company.

③　カリナさんは、{研究室|けんきゅうしつ}は{禁煙|きんえん}にしたほうがいいという{意見|いけん}を{持|も}っている。<br>Karina is of the opinion that it would be better to ban smoking in the office.

#### 4．…ように{言|い}う／{注意|ちゅうい}する／{伝|つた}える／{頼|たの}む

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ ように ＋ V（{言|い}う、{注意|ちゅうい}する、{伝|つた}える、{頼|たの}む）(say, warn, tell, ask)

This is used when reporting the details of an instruction or request. When the instruction or request is in direct speech, the sentence takes the form「～なさい」,「～てはいけません」or「～てください」:

①　{学生|がくせい}に{図書館|としょかん}で{物|もの}を{食|た}べないように{注意|ちゅうい}しました。<br>I warned the students not to eat in the library.<br>→　{学生|がくせい}に「{図書館|としょかん}で{物|もの}を{食|た}べてはいけません」と{注意|ちゅうい}しました。<br>I warned the students, saying, "You mustn't eat in the library."

②　この{仕事|しごと}を{今日中|きょうじゅう}にやるように{頼|たの}まれました。<br>I was asked to finish this job today.<br>→　「この{仕事|しごと}を{今日中|きょうじゅう}にやってください」と{頼|たの}まれました。<br>"Please finish this job today," I was asked.

③　{子|こ}どもたちに{早|はや}く{寝|ね}るように{言|い}いました。<br>I told the children to go to bed early.<br>→　{子|こ}どもたちに「{早|はや}く{寝|ね}なさい」と{言|い}いました。<br>I said to the children, "Go to bed early."

Note that「～なさい」is an expression indicating an instruction or order. It is only used in certain situations, such as when parents talk to their children. It is also used for instructions on examination papers and so on.

#### 5．～みたいだ・～みたいな～・～みたいに…（simile／illustration）

**Connection:** N ＋ みたいだ／みたいなN／みたいにV・いA・なA

「～ようだ」and「～みたいだ」mean the same, but「～みたいだ」is less formal:

①　わあ、このお{酒|さけ}、ジュースみたいだね。<br>Whoa! This cocktail tastes like juice, doesn't it!

②　わたしはジュースみたいなお{酒|さけ}しか{飲|の}まない。<br>I only drink cocktails that resemble juice.

③　このお{酒|さけ}はジュースみたいに{甘|あま}いよ。<br>Hey, this cocktail is as sweet as juice.

④　{夫|おっと}は、カレーみたいな{簡単|かんたん}な{料理|りょうり}しか{作|つく}れません。<br>My hubby can only cook easy stuff like curry.

> **Ref:** 「～のようだ・～のような～・～のように…」：<br>あの{病院|びょういん}はホテルのようだ。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 1）

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

#### ～ところ

「～ところ」means「～とき」(when), but it is only used in conjunction with certain words in polite situations, such as「お{忙|いそが}しいところ」(when you're busy),「お{休|やす}みのところ」(when you're resting),「お{急|いそ}ぎのところ」(when you're in a hurry) and「お{疲|つか}れのところ」(when you're tired). ## Lesson 3

#### 1．～（さ）せてもらえませんか・～（さ）せていただけませんか・～（さ）せてもらえないでしょうか・～（さ）せていただけないでしょうか

**Connection:** V（さ）せて ＋ もらえませんか／いただけませんか／もらえないでしょうか／いただけないでしょうか

These expressions are used when asking someone for permission to do something:

①　すみません。このパンフレットをコピーさせてもらえませんか。<br>Excuse me, may I copy this pamphlet?

②　{月曜日|げつようび}の{店長|てんちょう}{会議|かいぎ}で{報告|ほうこく}させていただけませんか。<br>Could I make a report at the store manager's meeting on Monday?

③　{一度|いちど}、{工場|こうじょう}を{見学|けんがく}させていただけないでしょうか。<br>I wonder if we might be allowed to tour the factory sometime?

「～させていただけませんか」is politer than「～させてもらえませんか」, and「～させていただけないでしょうか」is politer than「～させていただけませんか」:

> **Ref:** 「～させていただけませんか（polite request）」：しばらくここに{車|くるま}を{止|と}めさせていただけませんか。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 48）

#### 2．(1)　…ことにする

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ ことにする

「Vすることにする／Vしないことにする」is used to indicate a decision to do or not to do V:

①　{来年|らいねん}{結婚|けっこん}することにしました。<br>We've decided to get married next year.

②　{今晩|こんばん}は{外|そと}で{食事|しょくじ}をすることにしよう。<br>I have decided to eat out tonight.

#### 2．(2)　…ことにしている

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ ことにしている

「Vする／Vしないことにしている」indicates a habit one previously decided to or not to continue to do:

①　{毎週|まいしゅう}{日曜日|にちようび}の{夜|よる}は{外|そと}で{食事|しょくじ}をすることにしている。<br>We make it a rule to eat out on Sunday evening.

②　ダイエットしているので、お{菓子|かし}を{食|た}べないことにしている。<br>I make it a rule not to eat any sweet things, because I'm on a diet.

#### 3．(1)　…ことになる

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ ことになる

「Vする」and「Vしないことになる」indicate a decision that something is to happen or not.「ことにする」is used to focus on who is going to take an action, while「ことになる」is used to focus on an action or event that happens voluntarily:

①　{来月|らいげつ}アメリカへ{出張|しゅっちょう}することになりました。<br>(It has been decided that) I'm to go on a business trip to the States next month.

②　{中国|ちゅうごく}へは{田中|たなか}さんが{行|い}くことになるでしょう。<br>It will probably be Mr. Tanaka who is chosen to go to China.

However, even when one has made the decision oneself,「ことになる」may be used in order to play down the fact that it was one's own decision:

③　{部長|ぶちょう}、{実|じつ}は、{今年|ことし}の{秋|あき}に{結婚|けっこん}することになりました。{結婚式|けっこんしき}に{出席|しゅっせき}していただけないでしょうか。<br>Department Manager, I'm getting married this autumn, and I was wondering if you would be kind enough to come to the wedding.

#### 3．(2)　…ことになっている

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ ことになっている

When in the form「Vする／Vしないことになっている」, these expressions indicate that something has been scheduled, or established as a rule:

①　あしたの{朝|あさ}9{時|じ}から{試験|しけん}を{行|おこな}うことになっています。<br>A test is scheduled for 9:00 a.m. tomorrow.

②　うちでは{夜|よる}9{時|じ}{以降|いこう}はテレビをつけないことになっている。<br>In our house, the television is not switched on after 9:00 p.m.

#### 4．～てほしい・～ないでほしい

**Connection:** Vて-form／Vない-form（－ないで） ＋ ほしい

(1)「NにVてほしい」is used to indicate wishing that someone else (N) would do something (V):

①　わたしは{息子|むすこ}に{優|やさ}しい{人|ひと}になってほしいです。<br>I would like my son to become a kind person.

「Nに」can be omitted when N is known:

②　このごろ{自転車|じてんしゃ}を{利用|りよう}する{人|ひと}が{多|おお}いが、{規則|きそく}を{守|まも}って{乗|の}ってほしい。<br>Many people use bicycles these days, but I wish they would obey the regulations when they ride.

Wishing that N would not do V is shown by the negative form,「Vないでほしい」:

③　こんなところにごみを{捨|す}てないでほしい。<br>I wish people wouldn't dump rubbish in places like this.

Although they become expressions of request or instruction when used about someone else's actions, they would be too direct if used without any modification, so they are often softened by appending expressions like「のですが／んですが」:

④　すみません、ちょっと{手伝|てつだ}ってほしいんですが。<br>Excuse me, but I'd like you to help me for a moment if you could.

(2) They can also be used about matters other than people's actions, in which case,「Nが」is used instead of「Nに」:

⑤　{早|はや}く{春|はる}が{来|き}てほしい。<br>I wish spring would hurry up and come.

⑥　あしたは{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}らないでほしい。<br>I hope it doesn't rain tomorrow.

#### 5．(1)　～そうな～・～そうに…

**Connection:** Vます-form（－~~ます~~）／いA（－~~い~~）／なA（－~~な~~） ＋ そうなN／そうにV

「Vます－そうだ」has a different meaning when appended to a verb (「V－plain－そうだ」) than when appended to an adjective (「Aそうだ」).「Vそうだ」indicates that the speaker predicts that there is a high probability of V occurring, or indicates signs that V will occur:

①　ミラーさん、シャツのボタンが{取|と}れそうですよ。<br>Mr. Miller, one of your shirt buttons looks like it's about to come off.

②　{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}りそうなときは、{洗濯|せんたく}しません。<br>When it looks like rain, I don't do any washing.

「Aそうだ」means that something appears to be A:

③　ワンさんの{隣|となり}にいる{学生|がくせい}はまじめそうですね。<br>The student next to Mr. Wong appears serious-minded, doesn't he?

④　このケーキはおいしそうですね。<br>This cake looks yummy, doesn't it?

⑤　{子|こ}どもたちが{楽|たの}しそうに{遊|あそ}んでいます。<br>The children seem to be playing happily.

When「Vそうだ」(expressing a prediction, or an indication of a future event) and「Aそうだ」(indicating an external appearance) modify a noun, they take the form「そうなN」. When they modify a verb, they take the form「そうにV」:

⑥　{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}りそうなときは、{洗濯|せんたく}しません。<br>I don't do any washing when it looks like rain.

⑦　おいしそうなケーキがありますね。<br>There's a yummy-looking cake, isn't there?

⑧　{子|こ}どもたちが{楽|たの}しそうに{遊|あそ}んでいます。<br>The children seem to be playing happily.

> **Ref:** 「～そうだ（prediction, appearance）」：<br>{今|いま}にも{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}りそうです。<br>この{料理|りょうり}は{辛|から}そうです。<br>ミラーさんはうれしそうです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 43）

#### 5．(2)　～なさそう

**Connection:** いA（い→－く）／なA・N（~~だ~~→－では（じゃ）） ＋ なさそう

The negative form of「A／Nそうだ」is not「～ないそうだ」but「～なさそうだ」. It means that the article in question does not appear to be A or is not thought to be A:

①　あの{映画|えいが}はあまりおもしろくなさそうですね。<br>That film doesn't look very interesting, does it?

②　この{機械|きかい}はそんなに{複雑|ふくざつ}じゃ（では）なさそうです。<br>This machine doesn't look that complicated.

③　{彼|かれ}は{学生|がくせい}ではなさそうです。<br>He doesn't look like a student.

#### 5．(3)　～そうもない

**Connection:** Vます-form（－~~ます~~） ＋ そうもない

The negative form of「Vます－そうだ」is「Vます－そうもない」and indicates the prediction that V will probably not occur:

①　{今日|きょう}は{仕事|しごと}がたくさんあるので、5{時|じ}に{帰|かえ}れそうもありません。<br>I've got a lot of work to do today, so it doesn't look as if I'll be able to leave at five.

②　この{雨|あめ}はまだやみそうもないですね。<br>This rain doesn't look like stopping yet, does it?

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

#### ～たあと、…

**Connection:** Vたあと、…

「Vたあと、…」indicates the following on of a situation or circumstance (…) from V:

①　じゃ、{来週|らいしゅう}の{月曜日|げつようび}{会議|かいぎ}が{終|お}わった｛あと／あとで｝、お{会|あ}いしましょうか。<br>OK, shall we get together after the meeting has finished next Monday?

When「…」contains words like「いる」or「ある」,「あとで」sounds awkward:

## Lesson 4

#### 1．…ということだ（reported speech）

**Connection:** S (plain form) ＋ ということだ

(1)「Xということだ」is a reported-speech expression similar to「Xそうだ」and is used when reporting what someone else has said (X), or what people in general say:

①　{山田|やまだ}さんから{電話|でんわ}があったのですが、{約束|やくそく}の{時間|じかん}に{少|すこ}し{遅|おく}れるということです。<br>Mr. Yamada phoned; he said he would be a little late for the appointment.

②　{近|ちか}くにいた{人|ひと}の{話|はなし}によると、トラックから{急|きゅう}に{荷物|にもつ}が{落|お}ちたということです。<br>According to the bystanders, the load suddenly fell off the truck.

It can also take the form「とのことです」, but this tends to be more like written language:

③　（{手紙文|てがみぶん}）{先日|せんじつ}、ワンさんに{会|あ}いました。ワンさんから{先生|せんせい}によろしくとのことです。<br>(From a letter) I saw Mr. Wong the other day. He asked me to give you his regards.

(2)「Xということですね」can be used when repeating what someone has just said, in order to confirm it:

④　A：{部長|ぶちょう}に30{分|ぷん}ほど{遅|おく}れると{伝|つた}えてください。<br>Please tell the Department Manager that I'll be about thirty minutes late.<br>B：はい、わかりました。30{分|ぷん}ほど{遅|おく}れるということですね。<br>Yes, I understand. You'll be about thirty minutes late, right?

#### 2．…の・…の？

**Connection:** S (plain form) ＋ の／の？

This is an informal form of「…のですか」, used when conversing with someone you are on familiar terms with:

①　どこへ{行|い}くの？　Where are you off to?<br>…ちょっと{郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}へ。　I'm just popping down to the post office.

②　{元気|げんき}がないね。{先生|せんせい}にしかられたの？<br>You look a bit down in the dumps. Did your teacher tell you off?<br>…うん。　Yeah.

③　どうしたの？　What's up?<br>…お{母|かあ}さんがいないの。　Mum's not here.

> **Ref:** 「…のです・んです」is an expression used for emphasizing a cause, reason, logical basis or other explanation. While「…んです」is conversational,「…のです」is used in writing.　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 26）

#### 3．～ちゃう・～とく・～てる

**Connection:** Vてしまう → Vちゃう／Vておく → Vとく／Vている → Vてる

(1)「～てしまう」becomes「～ちゃう」in spoken language:

①　{行|い}ってしまいます → {行|い}っちゃいます<br>②　{読|よ}んでしまった → {読|よ}んじゃった<br>③　{見|み}てしまった → {見|み}ちゃった

(2)「～ておく」becomes「～とく」in spoken language:

④　{見|み}ておきます → {見|み}ときます<br>⑤　{作|つく}っておこう → {作|つく}っとこう<br>⑥　{読|よ}んでおいてください → {読|よ}んどいてください

(3)「～ている」becomes「～てる」in spoken language:

⑦　{走|はし}っている → {走|はし}ってる<br>⑧　{読|よ}んでいる → {読|よ}んでる<br>⑨　{見|み}ていない → {見|み}てない

#### 4．～（さ）せられる・～される（causative-passive）

**Connection:** VⅠ：ない-form（－~~ない~~）＋ せられる／される　　VⅡ：ない-form（－~~ない~~）＋ させられる　　VⅢ：する → させられる／{来|く}る → {来|こ}させられる

(1) This expression combines the causative and passive:

①　{太郎君|たろうくん}は{掃除|そうじ}をしました。　Taro cleaned up.<br>→　{先生|せんせい}は{太郎君|たろうくん}に{掃除|そうじ}をさせました。<br>(causative) The teacher made Taro clean up.<br>→　{太郎君|たろうくん}は{先生|せんせい}に{掃除|そうじ}をさせられました。<br>(causative-passive) Taro was made to clean up by the teacher.

(2) The sentence pattern「N₁はN₂にVさせられる」is the basic causative-passive form, but sometimes「N₂に」is not specified. However, in either case, it means that N₁ does V not through their own volition, but because they were made to do so by someone else:

②　{昨日|きのう}の{忘年会|ぼうねんかい}ではカラオケを｛{歌|うた}わせられた／{歌|うた}わされた｝。<br>I was made to sing a karaoke song at yesterday's year-end party.

③　この{会議|かいぎ}では{毎月|まいつき}{新|あたら}しい{問題|もんだい}について{研究|けんきゅう}したことを{発表|はっぴょう}させられます。<br>At this conference, we are made to present what we have found out about a new issue every month.

#### 5．～である（である style）

**Connection:** N／なA（－~~な~~） ＋ である　　～ている ＋ （の）である

「～である」means the same as「～だ」but is more formal. It is often used in writing, particularly for dissertations and the like:

①　「{失敗|しっぱい}は{成功|せいこう}の{母|はは}である」<br>Failure is the stepping-stone to success.

②　このような{事件|じけん}を{起|お}こしたことは{非常|ひじょう}に{残念|ざんねん}である。<br>To have brought about an affair like this is extremely regrettable.

③　ここは{去年|きょねん}まで{山|やま}であった。<br>This area was mountainous forest until last year.

In the「である」style,「～のだ」becomes「～のである」:

④　{世界中|せかいじゅう}の{人々|ひとびと}が{地球|ちきゅう}の{平和|へいわ}を{願|ねが}っているのである。<br>People all over the world are hoping for world peace.

#### 6．～~~ます~~、～~~ます~~、…・～く~~て~~、～く~~て~~、…（suspended form）

**Connection:** V：Vます-form（－~~ます~~）（います → おり）　いA：い → －く　＊なA：なA－で　＊N：N－で

(1) The suspended form of the verb (the same form as the ます-form) is used in the form「V₁（ます-form）、V₂」and marks a succession or concurrency of events, in the same way as「V₁（て-form）、V₂」:

①　{朝|あさ}{起|お}きたら、まず{顔|かお}を{洗|あら}い、コーヒーを{飲|の}み、{新聞|しんぶん}を{読|よ}みます。<br>After I get up in the morning, I first wash my face, then drink coffee and read the paper.

②　{彼|かれ}とは{学生|がくせい}{時代|じだい}、よく{遊|あそ}び、よく{話|はな}し、よく{飲|の}んだ。<br>I often fooled around, talked and went drinking with him when we were at university.

(2) The suspended form of「いる」is「おり」:

③　{兄|あに}は{東京|とうきょう}におり、{姉|あね}は{大阪|おおさか}にいます。<br>My elder brother is in Tokyo and my elder sister in Osaka.

(3) The suspended form of an adjective or noun indicates the coexistence of the meanings shown by each of the words:

④　マリアさんは、{優|やさ}しく、{頭|あたま}がよく、すばらしい{女性|じょせい}だ。<br>Maria is a kind, intelligent, wonderful woman.

#### 7．(1)　～（た）がる

**Connection:** Vます-form（－~~ます~~）＋ たがる　　いA（－~~い~~）／なA（－~~な~~） ＋ がる

When attached, in the form「Nが～（た）がる」, to an adjective expressing emotion, this indicates that N's (someone else's) emotion is revealing itself in their expression or behavior. When N appears to want something (「～たい」), it takes the form「～（た）がる」:

①　{太郎君|たろうくん}は{友達|ともだち}のおもちゃを{欲|ほ}しがる。<br>Taro covets his friends' toys.

②　このチームが{負|ま}けると、{息子|むすこ}はすごく{悔|くや}しがる。<br>My son will feel gutted if this team loses.

③　このごろの{若者|わかもの}は、{難|むずか}しい{本|ほん}を{読|よ}みたがらない。<br>Young people these days are disinclined to read difficult books.

#### 7．(2)　～（た）がっている

**Connection:** Vます-form（－~~ます~~）＋ たがっている　　いA（－~~い~~）／なA（－~~な~~） ＋ がっている

「～（た）がる」indicates a person's tendency to habitually behave in a way that demonstrates a particular emotion, aspiration, etc. When the person is behaving in that way at the present moment, the form「～（た）がっている」is used:

①　{太郎君|たろうくん}は{友達|ともだち}のおもちゃを{欲|ほ}しがっている。<br>Taro has got his eye on his friend's toys.

②　{好|す}きなチームが{負|ま}けて、{息子|むすこ}はすごく{悔|くや}しがっている。<br>The team he likes has lost, and my son is feeling gutted.

#### 8．…こと・…ということ

**Connection:** S (plain form) ＋ ［という］こと ＋ case particle　　＊なA ＋ なこと／であること

(1) When a particle or other part of speech is appended to a sentence, the form「…こと＋particle」is used to nominalize the sentence (make it function as a noun). The sentence that comes before「…こと」takes the plain form:

①　{田中|たなか}さんが{結婚|けっこん}したことを{知|し}っていますか。<br>Did you know that Mr. Tanaka has got married?

②　これは{田中|たなか}さんの{辞書|じしょ}ではないことがわかりました。<br>I realized that this is not Mr. Tanaka's dictionary.

When the sentence ends with a なA, the form「なA＋なこと」or「なA＋であること」is used:

③　{世界中|せかいじゅう}でこの{漫画|まんが}が{有名|ゆうめい}｛な／である｝ことを{知|し}っていますか。<br>Did you know that this manga is world-famous?

(2) When the sentence is long and complicated,「という」must be used in front of「こと」in order to nominalize the sentence. In this case,「～ということ」is appended to the plain-form sentence:

④　{二十歳|はたち}になればだれでも{結婚|けっこん}できるということを{知|し}っていますか？<br>Did you know that anyone can get married once they turn twenty?

⑤　{日本|にほん}に{来|き}てから、{家族|かぞく}はとても{大切|たいせつ}｛だ／である｝ということに{初|はじ}めて{気|き}がついた。<br>It was only after coming to Japan that I realized how important my family is.

⑥　この{辺|あた}りは{昔|むかし}、{海|うみ}｛だった／であった｝ということは、あまり{知|し}られていない。<br>It is not very well known that the area round here used to be under the sea.

> **Ref:** 「こと」：{朝|あさ}{早|はや}く{起|お}きることは{健康|けんこう}にいい。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 1）<br>{初|はじ}めて{好|す}きになった{人|ひと}のことを{覚|おぼ}えています。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 38）

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

「～の～」shows that N₁ and N₂ are identical. N₁ is a noun that shows an attribute of N₂, giving more information about it. It can also be expressed in the form「N₁であるN₂」:

①　{部長|ぶちょう}の{田中|たなか}をご{紹介|しょうかい}します。<br>I would like to introduce Mr. Tanaka, our Department Manager.

「たら」and the て-form can also be polite forms:

①　{会議|かいぎ}が{終|お}わりましたら、こちらからお{電話|でんわ}させていただきます。<br>I will take the liberty of telephoning you when the meeting has finished.

## Lesson 5

#### 1．(1)　あ～・そ～（contextual demonstrative noun (conversational)）

Demonstratives such as「あ～」and「そ～」can be used to refer to something that has cropped up in a conversation or appears in a text, as well as something that is physically present. In conversation, something directly known to both the speaker and the listener is indicated by「あ（あれ、あの、あそこ…）」. Something known to the speaker but not to the listener, or vice versa, is indicated by「そ（それ、その、そこ）」:

①　さっき、{山本|やまもと}さんに{会|あ}ったよ。　I met Mr. Yamamoto just now.<br>…え、あの{人|ひと}、{今|いま}{日本|にほん}にいるんですか。　Oh? Is he in Japan now?

②　さっき、{図書館|としょかん}でマリアさんという{人|ひと}に{会|あ}ったんだけどね。その{人|ひと}、この{学校|がっこう}で{日本語|にほんご}を{勉強|べんきょう}したんだって。<br>I met someone called Maria in the library just now. She said she had studied Japanese at this school.<br>…そうですか。その{人|ひと}は{何歳|なんさい}ぐらいですか。　Really? How old is she?

#### 1．(2)　そ～（contextual demonstrative pronoun (written)）

In sentences,「そ（それ、その、そこ…）」is used to refer to something that appeared in the previous sentence:

①　{会社|かいしゃ}を{出|で}たあと、{駅|えき}のレストランで{夕食|ゆうしょく}を{食|た}べました。そのとき、サイフを{落|お}としたんだと{思|おも}います。<br>After leaving the office, I had dinner in the station restaurant. I think I dropped my wallet then.

②　イギリスの{人気|にんき}{小説|しょうせつ}が{日本語|にほんご}に{翻訳|ほんやく}されました。それが{今年|ことし}{日本|にほん}でベストセラーになりました。<br>A popular British novel was translated into Japanese. Now it's become a best-seller in Japan this year.

#### 2．…んじゃない？

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~→な） ＋ ［んじゃないですか］／んじゃない？

「…んじゃないですか」is an informal form of「…のではありませんか」. It is used in informal conversation when saying what the speaker is thinking:

①　{元気|げんき}がないですね。{何|なに}か{困|こま}っていることがあるんじゃないですか。<br>You look a bit glum. Is something bothering you, I wonder?<br>…ええ、{実|じつ}は……。　Yes, as a matter of fact....

「んじゃないですか」is sometimes shortened to「んじゃない」when used with someone with whom the speaker is on familiar terms. In formal conversation, it lengthens to「のではないでしょうか」:

②　タワポンさん、{少|すこ}し{太|ふと}ったんじゃない。<br>Thawaphon, you've put on a little weight, haven't you?<br>…わかりますか。　Does it show?

#### 3．～たところに／で

**Connection:** V (motion verb) た-form ＋ ところ

Verbs indicating motion, such as「{行|い}く、{渡|わた}る、{曲|ま}がる、{出|で}る」(go, cross, turn, and emerge) are used in the form「Vた-form＋ところ」to indicate the position arrived at after the motion has taken place:

①　あの{信号|しんごう}を{左|ひだり}へ{曲|ま}がったところに、{郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}があります。<br>There's a post office just after you turn left at those traffic lights.

②　{改札|かいさつ}を{出|で}て、{階段|かいだん}を{上|のぼ}ったところで、{待|ま}っていてください。<br>Go through the ticket barrier and wait at the top of the steps, please.

#### 4．(1)(2)　～（よ）う（volitional form）とする／しない

**Connection:** V（よ）う ＋ とする／しない

(1)「V（よ）う（volitional form）とする／しない」indicates a situation that arises just before doing V, so「Vする」does not actually take place. When applied in this way, it is usually used together with「～とき」,「～たら」, etc.:

①　{家|いえ}を{出|で}ようとしたとき、{電話|でんわ}がかかってきた。<br>The phone rang as I was about to leave the house.

②　{雨|あめ}がやんだので、{桜|さくら}を{撮|と}ろうとしたら、カメラの{電池|でんち}が{切|き}れてしまった。<br>When I went to photograph the cherry blossoms because the rain had stopped, I found that my camera battery was flat.

(2) It can also indicate that someone is trying to do V:

③　{父|ちち}は{健康|けんこう}のためにたばこをやめようとしています。<br>My father is trying to give up smoking for the sake of his health.

④　あの{日|ひ}のことは、{忘|わす}れようとしても{忘|わす}れることができません。<br>I cannot forget what happened that day, even though I try.

(3)「V（volitional form）としない」indicates that a person does not intend to do V. It is usually used in reference to people other than oneself.

⑤　{妻|つま}は{紅茶|こうちゃ}が{好|す}きで、お{茶|ちゃ}やコーヒーを{飲|の}もうとしない。<br>My wife likes black tea, and she has no interest in drinking green tea, coffee or anything like that.

⑥　{人|ひと}の{話|はなし}を{聞|き}こうとしない{人|ひと}は、いつまでたっても{自分|じぶん}の{考|かんが}えを{変|か}えることができません。<br>People who are not interested in listening to what others say can never change their opinion.

#### 5．…のだろうか

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~→な） ＋ のだろうか

The expression「Xのだろうか」is used when asking oneself whether X is true or not. It can also be used with an interrogative such as「どう」,「{何|なに}」, or「いつ」to ask oneself a question:

①　この{店|みせ}ではクレジットカードが{使|つか}えるのだろうか。<br>I wonder if this shop takes credit cards.

②　{大学院|だいがくいん}に{入|はい}るためには、どうすればいいのだろうか。<br>I wonder what I need to do to get into postgraduate school.

It can also be used when asking someone else a question. Compared with「Xのですか」, which is more direct,「Xのでしょうか」is a gentler way of asking that does not demand an answer:

③　すみません。この{店|みせ}ではクレジットカードが{使|つか}えるのでしょうか。<br>Excuse me, does this shop take credit cards.

It can also be used in the form「Xのだろうか」, without an interrogative, when one wants to suggest that X is not true, or that one does not think X:

④　このクラスでは{日本語|にほんご}で{話|はな}すチャンスがとても{少|すく}ない。こんな{勉強|べんきょう}で{会話|かいわ}が{上手|じょうず}になるのだろうか。<br>There is very little opportunity to speak Japanese in this class. I wonder whether it is really possible to improve one's conversation skills with a study method like this?

#### 6．～との／での／からの／までの／への～

**Connection:** N ＋ ｛case particle ＋ の｝ ＋ N

When a word with a case particle such as「と、で、から、まで、or へ」attached is used to modify a noun,「の」is appended to the particle, except if the particle is「に」, in which case「に」is changed to「へ」and「への」is used:

①　{友達|ともだち}との{北海道|ほっかいどう}{旅行|りょこう}は、とても{楽|たの}しかったです。<br>The trip to Hokkaido with my friends was very enjoyable.

②　{日本|にほん}での{研究|けんきゅう}はいかがでしたか。<br>How did your research in Japan go?

③　{国|くに}の{両親|りょうしん}からの{手紙|てがみ}を{読|よ}んで、{泣|な}いてしまった。<br>I was moved to tears when I read the letter from my parents back in my home country.

④　{先生|せんせい}へのお{土産|みやげ}は{何|なに}がいいでしょうか。<br>I wonder what would make a good souvenir for our teacher?

「の」is not appended to「が」or「を」either:

⑤　{田中|たなか}さんの{欠席|けっせき}を{部長|ぶちょう}に{伝|つた}えてください。<br>Please report Mr. Tanaka's absence to the Department Manager.

⑥　{大学院|だいがくいん}で{医学|いがく}の{研究|けんきゅう}をするつもりです。<br>I intend to do medical research at postgraduate school.

#### 7．…だろう・…だろうと{思|おも}う（conjecture）

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ だろう

(1)「…だろう」is the plain form of「…でしょう」and is used in sentences in the plain style. It is used to express one's opinion conjecturally rather than definitely:

①　アジアの{経済|けいざい}はこれからますます{発展|はってん}するだろう。<br>The Asian economy will probably develop more and more from now on.

②　マリアさんの{話|はなし}を{聞|き}いて、ご{両親|りょうしん}もきっとびっくりされただろう。<br>I expect Maria's parents also got a shock when they heard what she had to say.

(2) In conversation, it is usual to append「と{思|おも}う」and use it in the form「…だろうと{思|おも}う」:

③　{鈴木君|すずきくん}はいい{教師|きょうし}になるだろうと{思|おも}います。<br>I think that young Suzuki will probably become a good teacher.

④　この{実験|じっけん}にはあと2、3{週間|しゅうかん}はかかるだろうと{思|おも}います。<br>I think that this experiment will probably take another two or three weeks.

> **Ref:** 「～でしょう？（asking for confirmation）」：<br>7{月|がつ}に{京都|きょうと}でお{祭|まつ}りがあるでしょう？　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 21）<br>「でしょう（conjecture）」：あしたは{雪|ゆき}が{降|ふ}るでしょう。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 32）

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

#### …から、～てください

**Connection:** V (polite form) ＋ から、Vてください

When used in this context,「…から」does not indicate a reason. It indicates some information that is a prerequisite for the request or instruction that follows:

①　お{金|かね}を{入|い}れるとボタンに{電気|でんき}がつきますから、それを{押|お}してください。<br>When you put some money in, the button lights up; then press that, please.

②　10{分|ぷん}ぐらいで{戻|もど}ってきますから、ここで{待|ま}っていてくれますか。<br>I'll be back in about ten minutes; could you wait here, please?

### {読|よ}む・{書|か}く

#### が／の

The「が」of the subject of a clause describing a noun can be replaced by「の」:

①　{留学生|りゅうがくせい}｛が／の｝かいた{絵|え}を{見|み}ました。<br>I saw the pictures that the foreign students had painted.

②　{田中|たなか}さん｛が／の｝{作|つく}ったケーキはとてもおいしかった。<br>The cake that Mr. Tanaka made was scrumptious.

## Lesson 6

#### 1．(1)　…て…・…って…（quoting）

**Connection:** S (plain form) ＋ て／って…

The「と」used for quoting can become「て」or「って」in spoken language:

①　{田中|たなか}さんは{昨日|きのう}{何|なん}て{言|い}っていましたか。　←「と」<br>What did Mr. Tanaka say yesterday?<br>…{今日|きょう}は{休|やす}むって{言|い}っていました。　←「と」<br>He said that he was going to take today off.

②　{店|みせ}の{前|まえ}に「{本日|ほんじつ}{休業|きゅうぎょう}」って{書|か}いてありました。　←「と」<br>There was a sign in front of the shop saying, 'Closed Today.'

The「～という」in「～という{名前|なまえ}をもつ{人|ひと}／もの／ところ」can also become「って」:

③　{昨日|きのう}、{田山|たやま}って{人|ひと}が{来|き}ましたよ。　←「という」<br>Someone called Tayama came by yesterday.

#### 1．(2)　～って…（topic）

**Connection:** S (plain form)／N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ って…

「Xって」is used when asking about something (X) that the speaker does not know much about, or when the speaker is describing the nature or characteristics of X:

①　ねえ、{函館|はこだて}って、どんな{町|まち}？<br>Say, what kind of city is Hakodate?

②　メンタルトレーニングっておもしろい！<br>This 'mental training' is fun!

#### 2．(1)　～つもりはない（negative intention）

**Connection:** V dic.-form ＋ つもりはない

(1)「～つもりはない」is the negative form of「～つもりだ」and means 'has no intention of':

①　{卒業後|そつぎょうご}は{就職|しゅうしょく}するつもりです。{大学院|だいがくいん}に{行|い}くつもりはありません。<br>After I graduate, I plan to get a job. I don't intend to go to postgraduate school.

②　{仕事|しごと}が{忙|いそが}しいので、{今夜|こんや}のパーティーに{出|で}るつもりはない。<br>I'm busy with work today, so I'm not thinking of going to the party tonight.

「Vつもりはない」can be changed to「そのつもりはない」if the details of what V indicates are known:

③　A：1{週間|しゅうかん}くらい{休|やす}みを{取|と}ったらどうですか。<br>How about taking a week or so off?<br>B：いえ、そのつもりはありません。<br>No, I'm not going to do that.

(2) There are two negative forms of「～つもりだ」:「～つもりはない」and「～ないつもりだ」.「～つもりはない」is a stronger negative, and is used when strenuously denying what the other party has said, for example:

④　{新|あたら}しいコンピューターが{発売|はつばい}されました。いかがですか。<br>A new type of computer has been brought out. How about buying one?<br>…コンピューターは{持|も}っているから｛○{買|か}うつもりはない／×{買|か}わないつもりだよ。｝<br>I've already got a computer, so I have absolutely no intention of buying another one.

#### 2．(2)　～つもりだった（past volition）

**Connection:** V dic.-form／Vない-form ＋ つもりだった

(1)「～つもりだった」is the past form of「～つもりだ」and means 'intended to':

①　{電話|でんわ}するつもりでしたが、{忘|わす}れてしまいました。すみません。<br>I meant to phone you, but I went and forgot. I'm sorry.

(2) It is often followed by words expressing a change of mind:

②　パーティーには{行|い}かないつもりでしたが、おもしろそうなので{行|い}くことにしました。<br>I hadn't intended to go to the party, but it looked like it would be fun, so I decided to go after all.

> **Ref:** 「つもりだ（volition）」：{国|くに}へ{帰|かえ}っても、{柔道|じゅうどう}を{続|つづ}けるつもりです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 31）

#### 2．(3)　～たつもり・～ているつもり

**Connection:** Vた-form／Vている／＊いA（－い）／＊なA（－な）／＊Nの ＋ つもり

「Xたつもり／Xているつもり」indicates that the agent thinks something is X. However, in reality, it may not be X at all, or it may be unknown whether it is X or not:

①　{外国語|がいこくご}を{練習|れんしゅう}するときは、{小|ちい}さな{子|こ}どもになったつもりで、{大|おお}きな{声|こえ}を{出|だ}してみるといい。<br>When practicing a foreign language, it is best to try speaking in a loud voice, as if one were a little child.

②　かぎがかかっていませんでしたよ。　It was unlocked, you know.<br>…すみません、かけたつもりでした。　Sorry. I thought I'd locked it.

③　わたしは{一生懸命|いっしょうけんめい}やっているつもりです。<br>I think I'm trying really hard.

④　{若|わか}いつもりで{無理|むり}をしたら、けがをしてしまった。<br>I went and injured myself, overdoing it as if I were young again.

⑤　{本当|ほんとう}の{研究|けんきゅう}{発表|はっぴょう}のつもりで、みんなの{前|まえ}で{話|はな}してください。<br>Please stand in front of the group and tell everyone, as if it were a real research presentation.

> **Ref:** 「V dic.-formつもりです（intention to act）」：<br>{国|くに}へ{帰|かえ}っても、{柔道|じゅうどう}を{続|つづ}けるつもりです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 31）

#### 3．～てばかりいる・～ばかり～ている

**Connection:** 1) Vて-form ＋ ばかりいる　　2) Nばかり ＋ Vt. ている

(1) This indicates that an action is performed habitually or repeatedly, and that the speaker is critical of or dissatisfied with this:

①　このネコは{一日中|いちにちじゅう}、{寝|ね}てばかりいる。<br>This cat just sleeps all day long.

②　{弟|おとうと}はいつもコンピューターゲームをしてばかりいる。<br>My younger brother is always playing computer games.

(2) With a transitive verb,「ばかり」can come immediately after what it refers to:

③　{弟|おとうと}はいつもコンピューターゲームばかりしている。<br>Almost all my younger brother does is play computer games.

#### 4．…とか…

**Connection:** N／S (plain form) ＋ とか

(1)「…とか…とか」is used when enumerating several similar examples:

①　{最近|さいきん}{忙|いそが}しくて、テレビのドラマとか{映画|えいが}とか{見|み}る{時間|じかん}がありません。

②　{健康|けんこう}のためにテニスとか{水泳|すいえい}とかを{始|はじ}めてみるといいですよ。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 36）

(2)「…」can be replaced by whole sentences:

③　{子|こ}どものとき、{母|はは}に「{勉強|べんきょう}しろ」とか「たくさん{食|た}べなさい」とかよく{言|い}われました。<br>When I was a child, I was often told by my mother to "Go and study," "Eat up," and so on.

④　{今日|きょう}のテストは「{難|むずか}しい」とか「{問題|もんだい}が{多|おお}すぎる」とか{思|おも}った{学生|がくせい}が{多|おお}いようです。<br>It seems that a lot of students thought that today's test was "difficult," "contained too many questions," and so forth.

⑤　やせたいんです。どうしたらいいですか。<br>I want to lose weight. How should I go about it?<br>…{毎日|まいにち}{水泳|すいえい}をするとか、ジョギングをするとかすればいいですよ。<br>You should do something like swimming or jogging every day.

#### 5．～てくる（advent of a condition）

**Connection:** Vて-form ＋ くる

「～てくる」indicates that a new event has happened, and that consequently something has become perceptible:

①　{暗|くら}くなって、{星|ほし}が{見|み}えてきた。<br>It grew dark, and the stars came out.

②　{隣|となり}の{家|いえ}からいいにおいがしてきた。<br>A good smell came wafting from the house next door.

#### 6．～てくる（approach）・～ていく（withdraw）

**Connection:** Vて-form ＋ くる／いく

「～てくる」and「～ていく」are appended to motion verbs to show the direction of motion.「～てくる」indicates that the motion is towards the speaker, and「～ていく」indicates that the motion is away from the speaker:

①　{兄|あに}が{旅行|りょこう}から{帰|かえ}ってきた。<br>My elder brother came back from his trip.

②　{授業|じゅぎょう}のあと、{学生|がくせい}たちはうちへ{帰|かえ}っていった。<br>After the lesson, the students went home.

### {読|よ}む・{書|か}く

#### こ～（contextual demonstrative pronoun）

「こ」in a sentence can indicate something that appears later in the sentence:

①　{新聞|しんぶん}にこんなことが{書|か}いてあった。{最近|さいきん}の{日本人|にほんじん}は{家族|かぞく}みんなで{休日|きゅうじつ}にコンピューターゲームを{楽|たの}しむそうだ。<br>This was written in the newspaper: "Apparently, Japanese families these days enjoy playing computer games together on their days off."

> **Ref:** 「あ～・そ～（contextual demonstrative pronoun (spoken)）」<br>「そ～（contextual demonstrative pronoun (written)）」　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 5）

## Lesson 7

#### 1．(1)　～なくてはならない／いけない・～なくてもかまわない

**Connection:** Vない-form（～~~ない~~）／＊いA（～~~い~~→く）／＊なA・N（で／は） ＋ なくてはならない／いけない・なくてもかまわない

(1)「～なくてはならない／いけない」indicates that「～」is obligatory or definitely necessary. It is the same as「～なければならない」:

①　この{薬|くすり}は{一日|いちにち}2{回|かい}{飲|の}まなくてはならない。<br>This medicine must be taken twice a day.

②　レポートは{日本語|にほんご}でなくてはなりません。<br>The report has to be in Japanese.

(2)「～なくてもかまわない」indicates that「～」is not necessary. It is a politer expression than「～なくてもいいです」:

③　{熱|ねつ}が{下|さ}がったら、{薬|くすり}を{飲|の}まなくてもかまわない。<br>Once your temperature has gone down, it's all right not to take the medicine.

④　{作文|さくぶん}は{長|なが}くなくてもかまいません。<br>The composition does not have to be very long.

> **Ref:** 「～なければならない（must be done regardless of whether the performer of the action wants to or not）」：{薬|くすり}を{飲|の}まなければなりません。<br>「～なくてもいい（no need to perform the action）」：あした{来|こ}なくてもいいです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 17）

#### 1．(2)　～なくちゃ／～なきゃ［いけない］

**Connection:** Vなくてはいけない → Vなくちゃ［いけない］　　Vなければいけない → Vなきゃ［いけない］

In informal conversation,「なくてはいけない」can become「なくちゃいけない」and「なければいけない」can become「なきゃいけない」.「いけない」may be omitted.

#### 2．…だけだ・［ただ］…だけでいい

**Connection:** 1) N ＋ だけ　　2) V・いA plain form／＊なA plain form（－だ→な） ＋ だけだ／だけでいい

(1)「～だけ」is attached to a noun to indicate a limit.　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 11）

①　{外国人|がいこくじん}の{社員|しゃいん}は{一人|ひとり}だけいます。

②　{休|やす}みは{日曜日|にちようび}だけです。

(2) A verb or an adjective can precede「…だけ」, creating a predicate:

③　{何|なに}をしているの？…ただ、{本|ほん}を{読|よ}んでいるだけです。<br>What are you doing?　I'm just reading a book.

④　{病気|びょうき}ですか？…ちょっと{気分|きぶん}が{悪|わる}いだけです。<br>Are you ill?　No, I'm just feeling a little poorly.

(3)「…するだけでいい」indicates that it is only necessary to perform the specified action (「…すること」) and nothing else:

⑤　{申|もう}し{込|こ}みはどうするんですか？…この{紙|かみ}に{名前|なまえ}を{書|か}くだけでいいんです。<br>How do I apply?　All you have to do is to write your name on this piece of paper.

#### 3．…かな（sentence-final particle）

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・＊N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ かな

(1)「…かな」is used when posing a question that does not demand an answer:

①　A：お{父|とう}さんの{誕生日|たんじょうび}のプレゼントは{何|なに}がいいかな。<br>I wonder what I should get Dad for his birthday?<br>B：セーターはどうかな。<br>How about a pullover, I wonder?

(2) Using「…ないかな」for an invitation or request has a softening effect, making the invitation or request less direct:

②　A：{明日|あす}みんなで{桜|さくら}を{見|み}に{行|い}くんですが、{先生|せんせい}もいっしょにいらっしゃらないかなと{思|おも}いまして。<br>We're all going to see the cherry blossoms tomorrow, and I wondered whether you might not like to come with us.<br>B：{桜|さくら}ですか。いいですね。<br>The cherry blossoms, eh? That would be nice.

③　A：3{時|じ}までにこの{資料|しりょう}を{全部|ぜんぶ}コピーしなければならないんだけど、{手伝|てつだ}ってくれないかな。<br>I have to get all of these materials copied by three o'clock, and I wondered whether you might not be able to help me.<br>B：いいよ。<br>No problem.

#### 4．(1)　～なんか…

**Connection:** N ＋ なんか

「～なんか」indicates that the speaker disparages「～」, attaching little importance to it. It is like「など」but is used in conversation:

①　わたしの{絵|え}なんかみんなに{見|み}せないでください。{絵|え}が{下手|へた}なんです。<br>Please don't go showing my pictures to everyone. I'm no good at painting.

#### 4．(2)　…なんて…

**Connection:** V・いA・なA plain form／N ＋ なんて

(1)「XなんてY」also indicates that the speaker disparages X and attaches little importance to it. It too is like「など」but is used in conversation:

①　わたしの{絵|え}なんてみんなに{見|み}せないでください。{絵|え}が{下手|へた}なんです。<br>Please don't go showing my pictures to everyone. I'm no good at painting.

(2)「Xなんて」is also used to express criticism of X or display surprise concerning it. It is used in conversation:

②　{昨日|きのう}、{大江|おおえ}さんという{人|ひと}から{電話|でんわ}があったよ。<br>You had a phone call from someone called Oe yesterday.<br>…{大江|おおえ}なんて（{人|ひと}）{知|し}りませんよ、わたし。<br>But I don't know anyone called Oe!

③　{先生|せんせい}が3{年|ねん}も{前|まえ}に{事故|じこ}にあって{亡|な}くなったなんて、{知|し}りませんでした。<br>I had no idea that our teacher had been killed in an accident and has been dead for three years!

④　{試験|しけん}に{一度|いちど}で{合格|ごうかく}できたなんて、びっくりしました。<br>I was amazed when I passed the test at my first attempt!

⑤　ミラーさんがあんなに{歌|うた}がうまいなんて、{知|し}りませんでした。<br>I had no idea that Mr. Miller was so good at singing!

「なんて」is used after a verb or adjective, as in ③, ④, and ⑤.「なんか」cannot be used in this case.

#### 5．(1)　～（さ）せる（emotional causative）

**Connection:** Vi. (emotion verb) ＋ （さ）せる

In addition to being used to express making someone else do something, the causative form「～（さ）せる」is also used to describe evoking an emotion. In this case, the verb used is an intransitive verb that indicates emotion, such as「{泣|な}く、びっくりする、{楽|たの}しむ or {驚|おどろ}く」(cry, be surprised, enjoy, be startled, etc.), and the person in whom the emotion is provoked is indicated by「を」:

①　{殴|なぐ}って、{弟|おとうと}を{泣|な}かせたことがある。<br>I sometimes hit my younger brother and made him cry.

②　テストで100{点|てん}を{取|と}って、{母|はは}をびっくりさせた。<br>I surprised my mother by scoring 100 points in the test.

> **Ref:** 「～（さ）せる（causative）」：{部長|ぶちょう}は{加藤|かとう}さんを{大阪|おおさか}へ{出張|しゅっちょう}させます。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 48）

#### 5．(2)　～（さ）せられる・～される（passive of emotional causative）

**Connection:** Vi. ＋ （さ）せられる

The emotional causative can also be used in the passive:

①　{何度|なんど}{買|か}っても{宝|たから}くじが{当|あ}たらず、がっかりさせられた。<br>I was disappointed when I never won anything in the lottery, no matter how many times I bought tickets.

②　{子|こ}どもが{書|か}いた{作文|さくぶん}はすばらしく、{感心|かんしん}させられた。<br>The composition the child wrote was excellent; I was impressed.

This usage indicates that the emotion (of surprise, sadness, disappointment, admiration, etc.) is strongly evoked.

> **Ref:** 「～（さ）せる（causative）」：{部長|ぶちょう}は{加藤|かとう}さんを{大阪|おおさか}へ{出張|しゅっちょう}させます。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 48）<br>「～（ら）れる（passive）」：わたしは{先生|せんせい}に{褒|ほ}められました。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 37）<br>「～（さ）せられる（causative-passive）」：{太郎君|たろうくん}は{先生|せんせい}に{掃除|そうじ}をさせられました。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 4）

#### 6．…なら、…

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ なら

「XならY」is used to recommend or ask about Y when the listener is thinking of doing X or is in a state of X. X can be a noun, verb or adjective.

「なら」is appended to the plain form, but when X ends in a なA or a noun, the form なA/noun＋なら is used:

①　パソコンを{買|か}いたいんですが。<br>…パソコンならパワー{電気|でんき}のがいいですよ。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 35）

②　ワインを{買|か}うなら、あの{酒屋|さかや}に{安|やす}くておいしいものがあるよ。<br>If it's wine you're buying, that off-licence has some that are good and cheap.

③　{日曜大工|にちようだいく}でいすを{作|つく}るなら、まず{材料|ざいりょう}に{良|よ}い{木|き}を{選|えら}ばなくてはいけません。<br>If you're going to make a chair by yourself, you have to start by choosing some good wood as a material.

④　{頭|あたま}が{痛|いた}いなら、この{薬|くすり}を{飲|の}むといいですよ。<br>If you've got a headache, you should take some of this medicine, you know.

⑤　{大学院|だいがくいん}への{進学|しんがく}のことを{相談|そうだん}するなら、どの{先生|せんせい}がいいかな。<br>If I'm going to get some advice about going on a postgraduate course, I wonder which professor I should talk to?

### {読|よ}む・{書|か}く

#### ～てくれ

(1)「Vてくれ」is used when you instruct or request something indirectly. When speaking directly, the form「～てください」is used:

#### 1．～てくる・～ていく（change）

(1)「～てくる」indicates arriving at a present situation through a process of change:

①　だんだん{春|はる}らしくなってきました。<br>Little by little, it became spring-like.

(2)「～ていく」indicates moving in the direction of a future change:

**Connection:** Vたら

(1)「～すること」is used when making a suggestion to someone by simply indicating a choice they could make.「～たらいかがですか」is a polite form of「～たらどう？」:

#### 3．…より…ほうが…（comparison）

**Connection:** ｛V・いA dic.-form／＊なA（－な）／＊N｝ ＋ より ＋ ｛V・＊いA dic.-form／なA（－な）／Nの｝ ＋ ほうが…

②　{今日|きょう}は、{北海道|ほっかいどう}より{東京|とうきょう}のほうが{気温|きおん}が{低|ひく}かったです。<br>The temperature was lower in Tokyo than in Hokkaido today.

⑤　{子|こ}どもに{食|た}べさせる{野菜|やさい}は、{値段|ねだん}が{安|やす}いより{安全|あんぜん}なほうがいい。<br>It is better for vegetables given to children to eat to be safe rather than cheap.

> **Ref:** 「～は～より（comparison）」：この{車|くるま}はあの{車|くるま}より{大|おお}きいです。<br>「～がいちばん～（the maximum of something indicated by an adjective）」：{日本|にほん}{料理|りょうり}［の{中|なか}］で{何|なに}がいちばんおいしいですか。<br>…てんぷらがいちばんおいしいです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 12）

**Connection:** Nらしい

「N₁らしいN₂」indicates that N₂ possesses a typical quality or property of N₁:

**Connection:** V・＊いA plain form／なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ らしい

(1)「…らしい」indicates that「…」is reported speech (information that has been read or heard):

**Connection:** Vない-form（－~~ない~~） ＋ ず、…

(1)「～ている」indicates a historical fact, experience, record or history. It is often used with an adverb expressing frequency or duration, such as「～{回|かい}」(times), or「{長|なが}い{間|あいだ}」(for a long time):

## Lesson 12

#### 1．…もの／もんだから

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・＊N plain form（－~~だ~~→な） ＋ もの／もんだから

「…もの／もんだから」indicates the reason or cause of something:

①　{急|いそ}いでいたものですから、かぎをかけるのを{忘|わす}れてしまいました。<br>I was in a rush, so I went and forgot to lock up.

②　とても{安|やす}かったものだから、{買|か}いすぎたんです。<br>It was very cheap, so I bought too much.

「XものだからY」is sometimes used when an undesirable event (Y) has occurred, to justify it, or give a reason for it in order to deny responsibility and exculpate oneself:

③　A：どうしてこんなに{遅|おそ}くなったんですか。　Why are you so late?<br>B：すみません。{出|で}かけようとしたら、{電話|でんわ}がかかってきたものですから。<br>I'm sorry; the telephone rang when I was about to leave.

It is not appropriate to use「…ものだから」in the same way as「から」and「ので」to indicate an objective cause or reason:

④　この{飛行機|ひこうき}は1{時間|じかん}に300キロ{飛|と}ぶ｛○から／○ので／×ものだから｝、3{時間|じかん}あれば{向|む}こうの{空港|くうこう}に{着|つ}く。<br>This aircraft flies at 300 km/hr, so we can get to our destination airport in three hours.

> **Ref:** 「…から（reason）」：<br>どうして{朝|あさ}、{新聞|しんぶん}を{読|よ}みませんか。…{時間|じかん}がありませんから。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 9）

#### 2．(1)　～（ら）れる（indirect passive (intransitive verb)）

In addition to direct passive sentences, in which the object Y of the transitive verb V of the active sentence「Xが（は）YをVする」becomes the subject of the passive sentence, Japanese passive sentences also include ones which take the indirect object Y of the active sentence「Xが（は）YにVする」as their subject, and also ones which take the owner Y of the object Z of the transitive verb V of the active sentence「XがYのZをVする」as their subject:

①　{先生|せんせい}はわたしを{注意|ちゅうい}した。（を → が（は））<br>→ わたしは{先生|せんせい}に{注意|ちゅうい}された。

②　{部長|ぶちょう}はわたしに{仕事|しごと}を{頼|たの}んだ。（に → が（は））<br>→ わたしは{部長|ぶちょう}に{仕事|しごと}を{頼|たの}まれた。

③　{泥棒|どろぼう}がわたしの{財布|さいふ}を{盗|ぬす}んだ。（の → が（は））<br>→ わたしは{泥棒|どろぼう}に{財布|さいふ}を{盗|ぬす}まれた。　（①～③☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 37）

In Japanese, it is also possible to make a passive sentence from an intransitive verb sentence, i.e.,「Xが（は）Vする」. In this case, the person affected by the action X becomes the subject of the passive sentence, indicating that he or she has been adversely affected by (i.e., has been inconvenienced or harmed by) the action:

④　{昨日|きのう}{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}った。(intransitive verb)　It rained yesterday.<br>→ わたしはきのう{雨|あめ}に{降|ふ}られて、ぬれてしまった。(passive of intransitive verb)<br>I was rained on yesterday and got wet.

⑤　あなたがそこに{立|た}つと、{前|まえ}が{見|み}えません。(intransitive verb)<br>→ あなたにそこに{立|た}たれると、{前|まえ}が{見|み}えません。(passive of intransitive verb)<br>If you stand there, I can't see what's in front of me.

The owner of the subject of an intransitive verb can also become the subject of a passive sentence:

⑥　わたしの{父|ちち}が{急|きゅう}に{死|し}んで、わたしは{大学|だいがく}に{行|い}けなかった。(intransitive verb)<br>My father died suddenly, and I couldn't go to university.<br>→ わたしは{父|ちち}に{急|きゅう}に{死|し}なれて、{大学|だいがく}に{行|い}けなかった。(passive of intransitive verb)<br>I was affected by my father's death, and I couldn't go to university.

#### 2．(2)　～（ら）れる（indirect passive (transitive verb)）

A transitive verb can also be used in a passive sentence indicating that its subject has been inconvenienced or harmed:

①　こんなところに{信号|しんごう}を{作|つく}られて、{車|くるま}が{渋滞|じゅうたい}するようになってしまった。<br>They went and put some signals in a place like this, and now the traffic gets jammed up.

②　わたしの{家|いえ}の{前|まえ}にゴミを{捨|す}てられて{困|こま}っています。<br>I've got a problem with rubbish being dumped in front of my house.

> **Ref:** 「～（さ）せられ／せられ（causative-passive）」：{太郎君|たろうくん}は{先生|せんせい}に{掃除|そうじ}をさせられた。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 4）

#### 3．～たり～たり

**Connection:** Vたり　　いA → －~~い~~かったり　　なA → －なだったり　　N＋だ → －~~だ~~だったり

(1)「～たり～たり」is used for giving approximately two suitable examples from among a number of actions:

①　{休|やす}みの{日|ひ}は、{洗濯|せんたく}をしたり、{掃除|そうじ}をしたりします。(listing of actions)　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 19）

(2) In the construction「V₁たりV₂たり」, V₁ and V₂ can be verbs with opposite meanings, to express the fact that V₁ and V₂ are occurring alternately:

②　{映画|えいが}を{見|み}ているとき{笑|わら}ったり{泣|な}いたりしました。

③　この{廊下|ろうか}は{人|ひと}が{通|とお}ると、{電気|でんき}がついたり{消|き}えたりします。

「～たり～たり」can also be connected to adjectives to show that a number of different types of something exist:

④　この{店|みせ}の{食|た}べ{物|もの}は{種類|しゅるい}が{多|おお}くて、あまかったりからかったりします。<br>This restaurant has a lot of dishes on the menu, including both sweet ones and spicy ones.

#### 4．～っぱなし

**Connection:** Vます-form（－~~ます~~） ＋ っぱなし

「～っぱなし」indicates the undesirably lengthy continuation of a situation that normally would be terminated promptly:

①　{服|ふく}が{脱|ぬ}ぎっぱなしだ。かたづけないから、{部屋|へや}がきたない。<br>You just take your clothes off and leave them lying around. You don't put them away, so your room is untidy.

②　こらっ。ドアが{開|あ}けっぱなしだよ。{早|はや}く{閉|し}めなさい。<br>Hey, you've left the door open. Hurry up and shut it.

> **Ref:** 「…まま、…／～のまま、…」：<br>{眼鏡|めがね}をかけたまま、お{風呂|ふろ}に{入|はい}った。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{中級|ちゅうきゅう}Ⅰ』Lesson 8）

#### 5．(1)　…おかげで、…・…おかげだ

**Connection:** V・＊いA plain form／なA plain form（－~~だ~~→な）／N plain form（－~~だ~~→の） ＋ おかげで／おかげだ

「XおかげでY／Xおかげだ」is used when a favorable result (Y) is produced by a particular cause (X):

①　{先生|せんせい}が{手紙|てがみ}を{書|か}いてくださったおかげで、{大|おお}きい{病院|びょういん}で{研修|けんしゅう}を{受|う}けられることになった。<br>Thanks to my teacher writing a letter, I was able to get a training place at a big hospital.

②　{値段|ねだん}が{安|やす}かったおかげで、たくさん{買|か}えました。<br>Thanks to the price being low, I was able to buy a lot.

③　{地図|ちず}の{説明|せつめい}が{丁寧|ていねい}なおかげで、{待|ま}ち{合|あ}わせの{場所|ばしょ}がすぐにわかりました。<br>Because the map was explained to me so carefully, I was easily able to find the meeting place.

④　{皆様|みなさま}のおかげで、スピーチ{大会|たいかい}で{優勝|ゆうしょう}することができました。<br>Thanks to everyone's support and encouragement, I was able to win the speech contest.

#### 5．(2)　…せいで、…・…せいだ

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA plain form（－~~だ~~→な）／N plain form（－~~だ~~→の） ＋ せいで／せいだ

Conversely, when an unfavorable result is produced,「…せいで」is used:

①　{事故|じこ}のせいで、{授業|じゅぎょう}に{遅|おく}れてしまった。<br>I was late to the lesson because of the accident.

②　｛かぜ{薬|ぐすり}を{飲|の}んでいる／かぜ{薬|ぐすり}の｝せいで、{眠|ねむ}くなった。<br>Because ｛I had taken some cold medicine ／ of the cold medicine｝, I felt sleepy.

### {話|はな}す・{聞|き}く

#### …みたいです（conjecture）

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／＊なA・N plain form（－~~だ~~） ＋ みたいだ

「…みたいです」indicates a guess from how a situation looks (e.g., something's external appearance):

①　{電気|でんき}が{消|き}えているね。{隣|となり}の{部屋|へや}は{留守|るす}みたいだね。<br>The lights are out. It looks like the guys next door are out.

②　{田中|たなか}さんはお{酒|さけ}を{飲|の}んだみたいです。{顔|かお}が{赤|あか}いです。<br>It looks like Mr. Tanaka has been drinking. His face is red.

「…みたいです」means the same as「ようだ」, which is used in writing or formal speech:

③　{資料|しりょう}が{届|とど}いたようですので、{事務室|じむしつ}に{取|と}りに{行|い}ってまいります。<br>It looks like the documents have arrived, so I'll go to the office to get them.

> **Ref:** 「…ようだ（guessing from a situation）」：<br>{隣|となり}の{部屋|へや}にだれかいるようです。　（☞『みんなの{日本語|にほんご}{初級|しょきゅう}Ⅱ』Lesson 47）

### {読|よ}む・{書|か}く

#### どちらかと{言|い}えば、～ほうだ

**Connection:** V・いA plain form／なA plain form（－だ→な） ＋ ほうだ

「どちらかと{言|い}えば、Xほうだ」is used to indicate that a subject is strictly speaking, not completely X, but could be described as being X on the whole, without definitely committing oneself as to whether it is X or not:

①　この{辺|あた}りには{高|たか}い{店|みせ}が{多|おお}いのですが、この{店|みせ}はどちらかと{言|い}えば{安|やす}いほうです。<br>Most of the shops round here are expensive, but this one is on the cheaper side.

②　わたしはどちらかと{言|い}えば、{好|す}き{嫌|きら}いはあまりないほうだ。<br>I am someone who tends not to have strong likes and dislikes.

③　この{町|まち}はわたしの{国|くに}ではどちらかと{言|い}えば、にぎやかなほうです。<br>In my country, this town would be considered to lean toward the lively side.

④　{食事|しょくじ}しながらお{酒|さけ}を{飲|の}みますか。　Do you drink alcohol with your meals?<br>…そうですね。いつもではありませんが、どちらかと{言|い}えば{飲|の}むほうですね。<br>Let me see now. Not always, but I suppose I do tend to.

#### ～ますように・ませんように

(1)「～ますように／～ませんように」indicates wishing, hoping or praying that something will or will not happen. It is often used together with「どうか」or「どうぞ」when talking to oneself, or as a warning to someone else:

①　{優|やさ}しい{人|ひと}と{結婚|けっこん}できますように。<br>I hope I can get married to someone nice.

②　どうか{大|おお}きい{地震|じしん}が{起|お}きませんように。<br>I hope there won't be any big earthquakes.

③　{先生|せんせい}もどうぞ{風邪|かぜ}をひかれませんように。<br>I do hope you won't catch a cold, either, Sir.
